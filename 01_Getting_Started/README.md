# Session 1 — Getting Started with R

## Learning goals

By the end of this session, you can create **character and numeric vectors**, combine vectors into a **data frame**, inspect its structure, filter and sort rows, and draw simple barplots.

## Before you start

Install R and RStudio using the [setup guide](../SETUP.md).

Open [01_PubMed_Exercise.R](01_PubMed_Exercise.R) in RStudio and save a personal copy as **`01_PubMed_Exercise_YourName.R`** (replace *YourName* with your name). Work directly in that copy. Complete each `...` or placeholder. Run a line or selection using **Ctrl+Enter** (Windows) or **Cmd+Enter** (macOS).

## Your investigation

1. Select **one environmental compound** and **up to six** associated health risks or diseases.
2. In [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), search for your compound together with each disease, for example `"particulate matter" AND "asthma"`. Write down the number of results.
3. Select **a different compound** and search for it with the **same diseases in the same order**, for example `"arsenic" AND "asthma"`.
4. Complete Tasks 1–7 in your R script. Use your own search counts, not invented numbers.

**Record the date of your PubMed searches.** Search counts change as new papers are published, and results depend on your search terms.

## Questions for discussion

Which health outcome has the most publications for each compound? Does a higher number of publications mean a compound causes that disease, or is more harmful?

**Save and keep your completed `01_PubMed_Exercise_YourName.R` script.**
